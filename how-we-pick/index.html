How we pick

How we approach recommendations.

A recommendation is only useful if you understand why it is being made.

At The Buying Bureau Co., we look at the things that are most likely to matter to someone actually considering a purchase: what the product or service is designed to do, how it works, what it costs, what is included, where the limitations are, and who is most likely to get value from it.

Our research may include product specifications, company documentation, demonstrations, pricing information, independent sources, expert guidance where relevant, and patterns in customer feedback. The exact mix depends on what we are writing about.

We do not assume that every product we cover has been personally tested. When we have hands-on experience with something, we will say so. When an article is based on research rather than personal use, we will not write as though we have used the product ourselves.

We also try to separate a product’s marketing claims from what can reasonably be supported. If a claim seems exaggerated, unclear, or heavily dependent on individual results, we would rather qualify it than repeat it as fact.

We care about fit, not just whether something is “good”

A product does not have to be bad for us to tell some readers to skip it.

Price, complexity, ongoing costs, learning curve, alternatives and individual needs can all change whether something makes sense. That is why we try to explain both who a product may suit and who may be better off looking elsewhere.

Our affiliate relationships

Some products and services we cover may pay us a commission when a reader purchases through one of our links.

Those relationships do not buy a favorable recommendation or a particular ranking.

We may cover products that do not have an affiliate program, and we may decide that an affiliate product is not a good fit for certain readers.

If we ever publish sponsored content or accept payment specifically for a placement, it will be clearly identified as such.

Useful before profitable

An article should still be useful even if you never click a link.

That is the standard we are aiming for.
